## 3. Answers to the five questions

**Q1. Is Pink Sheet GOLD republishable under CC BY 4.0, or does the third-party exception apply? What source is named?**

- CONFIRMED FACT: the Dataset Terms give CC BY 4.0 "unless specifically labeled otherwise" and add an exception: some datasets "are provided by third parties, and may not be redistributed or reused without the consent of the original data provider, or may be subject to terms and conditions that are different". Summary terms: the conditions can be "indicated ... in the data or indicator metadata".
- CONFIRMED FACT: the workbook is not labelled with a licence or terms of use in any sheet we could read (Monthly Indices, Description, Index Weights and Mismatch Details contained no licence, copyright or permission text). The Commodity Markets page only links to the terms pages.
- CONFIRMED FACT: the workbook's Description sheet lists these sources in the cell run for the gold entry: Bloomberg Finance L.P.; Kitco.com; International Monetary Fund (International Financial Statistics); London Bullion Market; Metals Week; Platts Metals Week; Shearson Lehman Brothers (Metal Market Weekly Review); Thomson Reuters Datastream; World Bank. It does **not** name IBA or "LBMA Gold Price"; "London Bullion Market" is named. Definition: spot average of daily rates from June 2025, previously London afternoon fixing, 99.5% fine.
- TECHNICAL DEFICIENCY: our parser flattens a row into joined cell text, so the exact column mapping of that source list to the gold row (versus the whole Precious Metals block) is **not verified**; treat it as "the sources listed in the Description sheet for the precious metals entry".
- OPEN LEGAL QUESTION: whether these third-party names (a list of several commercial providers) make GOLD "third-party data" under the exception, and whose consent is required, is not answerable from the text. The World Bank must say.

**Q2. What do IBA/LBMA terms say about derived indices and use via a third party such as the World Bank?**

- CONFIRMED FACT: IBA offers usage, historical access, redistribution and "Derived Benchmark Licenses (e.g. for the creation of derived indices)" and its 2026 fee schedule contains an "LBMA Precious Metals Derived Index Licence" with fees charged per benchmark. The schedule addresses any entity or person that "uses new or historical LBMA Gold Price" information in valuation (sentence truncated by the 25-word limit) and vendors/redistributors that "redistribute new or historical LBMA Gold Price" information (a licence is required, per the truncated sentence). Exact scope wording beyond these fragments was not retained.
- CONFIRMED FACT: LBMA states since 24 Nov 2025 that historical LBMA tables sit behind a portal, that "a licence from IBA is required in order to obtain, use or redistribute real-time or historical benchmark data", and that non-commercial or educational users may access the tables after self-certification which LBMA will "cross-check with the licence provider".
- Not found in any retrieved text: a statement on data obtained from the World Bank or any other third party, a statement that third-party-sourced monthly averages are covered or exempt, and a definition of "derived index" that would include a dashboard ratio. These are OPEN LEGAL QUESTIONS.
- ASSUMPTION (unverified, do not rely): the World Bank may hold a licence covering its own publication; nothing read confirms this or its sub-licensing scope.

**Q3. ECB EXR.D.USD.EUR.SP00.A monthly means: suitability and reuse.**

- CONFIRMED FACT: free reuse "on the condition that the source is quoted (e.g. 'Source: ECB statistics.') and that the statistics (including metadata) are not modified"; third-party data are excluded; "irrespective of any subsequent commercial or non-commercial use" appears in the free-access statement. The disclaimer page adds: if modified by the user "this must be stated explicitly". The reference rates are "published for information purposes only", "strongly discouraged" for transactions, and "intended for information purposes only" in the framework PDF.
- Our use: a monthly mean of the daily series (own calculation) and a USD/EUR conversion of a gold price. CONFIRMED FACT that attribution and an explicit own-calculation statement are required (already in the attribution text of `GOLD_PUBLICATION_DECISION.md`). OPEN LEGAL QUESTION (minor): whether aggregating counts as "modified" in the sense of the usage policy, which is written about publishing the statistics themselves. ASSUMPTION: labelling the result as own calculation satisfies the disclaimer wording. Suitability for an informational dashboard indicator is consistent with "information purposes only"; a non-reliance line is advisable (design suggestion, not a requirement found).
- Not retrieved: any ECB sentence on commercial reuse specific to the reference rates beyond the general policy.

**Q4. Back-calculation risk.**

- CONFIRMED FACT (arithmetic from the repo's own formulas; `etl/static_export.py` publishes `gold_grams_per_house` and `gold_index` as full-precision floats; `house_eur` is already public): `gold_eur_oz(t) = house_eur(t) * 31.1034768 / gold_grams_per_house(t)`. The public JSON therefore reconstructs the monthly EUR/oz series essentially exactly, without any extra input. Multiplying by the public ECB monthly mean gives USD/oz. `gold_index` alone gives the relative path (`gold_eur(t) = gold_index(t)/100 * gold_eur(2000-01)`), and the anchor follows from the other field.
- Consequence: keeping `gold_eur_oz` `null` and not publishing the workbook does **not** prevent a reader from recovering the World Bank monthly series. The minimal variant reduces direct distribution but not reconstructability. This is a **technical fact**, not a mitigation.
- No retrieved term addresses reconstruction. The third-party clause speaks of "redistributed or reused"; whether recoverable derived data amounts to redistribution is an OPEN LEGAL QUESTION. Options for the owner (each is a ROOD matter, not proposed here as a decision): publish only one of the two fields; or round/coarsen published values; or obtain the confirmation in section 5. None of these is recommended or assessed for legal effect here.

**Q5. What written confirmation or licence is needed?** See section 5. In short: a written statement from the World Bank on its authority for GOLD, and, only if the World Bank cannot give it, an IBA statement on whether this use needs a usage, redistribution or "LBMA Precious Metals Derived Index Licence".

## 5. Smallest question and evidence that would let the owner decide

1. **The one question to the World Bank** (already drafted in `docs/governance/GOLD_PUBLICATION_DECISION.md`; the retrieved evidence sharpens it): the workbook lists Bloomberg Finance L.P., Kitco.com, the IMF (IFS), London Bullion Market, Metals Week, Platts Metals Week, Shearson Lehman Brothers, Thomson Reuters Datastream and the World Bank as sources and defines GOLD as the spot average of daily rates since June 2025 (previously the London afternoon fixing). Ask: (a) is GOLD covered by CC BY 4.0 or by the third-party exception; (b) if third-party, who is the rights holder and is the World Bank authorised to permit public display of two monthly derived series (EUR index with 2000-01 = 100; grams of gold per CBS average home price); (c) is the attribution "The World Bank: Commodity Price Data (Pink Sheet): Gold, USD per troy ounce" sufficient.
2. **Evidence that would suffice for the owner:** a written reply from the World Bank that (i) GOLD is under CC BY 4.0 for this use, or (ii) names the rights holder and the permission needed. If (ii), the second question to IBA is whether this use needs an LBMA Precious Metals Derived Index or redistribution licence, and which.
3. **Decision for the owner after the answer** (not made here): keep the current limited GO, narrow it, or revert to the HOLD. A reply that the derived series are fine removes B1 and B2; a reply that a licence is needed returns the matter to the owner as a ROOD cost/scope decision.
4. If no answer is sought, the position is unchanged: the limited GO of 2026-10-19 is an owner risk acceptance, "no legal proof of unlimited rights".
